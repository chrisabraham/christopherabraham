# Custom closet brand: separating the company from its first showroom

A custom closet and wardrobe company with showrooms in several states ran everything from one website. Search engines kept merging the brand with its original showroom, and its location pages, Google Business Profiles, and town pages stepped on each other.

## The engagement

A fixed-price project in milestones, delivering recommendations and structured data ready for the developer to install.

## What I delivered

### A clear entity model

The company described as the parent organization, and each showroom as a branch of it, so search engines and AI assistants can tell which is the brand and which is one of its locations. The JSON-LD states that relationship explicitly.

### Organization and product markup

Home page and product JSON-LD, validated and ready to install.

### Location pages matched to profiles

Each Google Business Profile linking to a page about that specific showroom, rather than to the home page.

### Profiles and town pages with separate jobs

Guidance for areas where a profile and a town page cover the same ground, so they reinforce each other.

### Services, regions, and breadcrumbs

A structure for how services, regions, and showrooms link together, reflected in breadcrumb markup.

## Why it matters

When every page and listing describes the business the same way, search engines can't tell the locations apart, and each one competes with the others. Distinct entities and distinct pages let every showroom rank in its own area while the brand ranks for itself.
