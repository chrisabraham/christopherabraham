# How to choose the next SEO fix when developer time is scarce

By [Christopher Abraham](https://christopherabraham.com/about/) · Published October 24, 2026

An audit can produce a hundred findings while the development team has room for two changes. The useful question is which two deserve the available time. Counting warnings will not answer it.

I organize that decision around the pages the business depends on, the strength of the diagnosis, and the work required to deliver a change. A priority list is a set of choices under constraints. It should explain why something moves first and what evidence would change that choice.

## Identify the pages that matter to the business

Begin with a short inventory of useful destinations: the services people inquire about, products currently available to buy, location pages for operating branches, and articles that support a real customer decision. Ask the owner which actions make those pages valuable.

Traffic alone can mislead. An old article may bring many visits without producing relevant inquiries, while a specialist service page attracts a small audience with a strong reason to call. Avoid inventing a monetary value for either. If conversion evidence is absent, record the owner's explanation and label it as a business judgment.

Use Search Console to examine search visibility alongside that inventory. Google's [introduction to Search Console](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start) describes its crawl, indexing, and performance reports. Those reports supply observations about search; they do not establish the value of a lead or identify every cause of a change.

## Separate active failures from improvement opportunities

A broken inquiry form on an important service page needs a different conversation from a title that could be clearer. Likewise, an accidental exclusion affecting a whole product category deserves investigation before a cosmetic improvement to an infrequently used archive.

Use three practical queues:
- **Restore:** an important customer action or access path has failed.
- **Investigate:** evidence suggests a material problem, but the cause or scope remains uncertain.
- **Improve:** the current experience works, and a bounded change could make it more useful.

These queues prevent an attractive improvement from displacing an unresolved failure. They also avoid treating every suspicion as an emergency. A sudden graph change may justify checking the data before changing the website.

## Compare candidates without false precision

For each candidate, record five fields: business relevance, affected population, confidence in the diagnosis, implementation effort, and dependencies. Use plain descriptions. “Shared template serving the current product catalog” is more informative than an unexplained impact score of nine.

Confidence concerns the explanation, not the confidence of the person presenting it. A reproducible failure with a known template cause is different from a correlation between a release and falling visits. Give the second case an investigation task that could confirm or reject the theory.

Effort includes review, release coordination, content preparation, and verification. A ten minute code edit that needs a vendor's approval and an overnight maintenance window is not a ten minute delivery. Ask the developer for an estimate before ranking the task as an easy win.

A simple high, medium, or low label can help sort a discussion, provided its meaning is written down. Avoid multiplying arbitrary scores into a precise looking return forecast. The ordering is a decision aid, not a prediction of future rankings.

## Work through a small hypothetical backlog

Imagine a supplier with one developer afternoon available. Three findings arrive: the quote form fails on mobile, twenty product pages have similar descriptions, and an old archive has short meta descriptions. These are invented examples, not reported client results.

The quote form belongs in Restore if testing reproduces the failure. Fixing it protects the customer action that existing visits are supposed to support. The product descriptions need more context: do the products serve different needs, and is important specification information absent? That may become an editorial assignment before any code work is required.

The archive descriptions can wait unless someone establishes a stronger business reason. A crawler highlighting them in red does not change that. The outcome of the afternoon should be a repaired and verified quote path, plus a clearly assigned investigation of the product pages.

If the form failure turns out to be a test configuration problem, revise the queue immediately. Good prioritization leaves room for new evidence rather than defending yesterday's order.

## Make each selected task small enough to finish

Turn “improve product SEO” into a bounded assignment: inspect one product family, identify missing information, decide which template fields are needed, and verify the revised output on named examples. The next task depends on that result.

Assign one owner and specify the closing evidence. A title change needs the expected title on the relevant production URLs. A redirect repair needs the intended destination and response behavior for the agreed source set. A form repair needs a successful submission and confirmed receipt, following the [page to inbox inquiry test](https://christopherabraham.com/guides/test-website-inquiries/).

Do not require a ranking increase to close a development ticket. That would leave a technically complete change permanently open while search performance moves for many reasons. Track implementation and business measurement in separate fields.

## Review the queue after each release

Keep a compact change log: what changed, where, when, who checked it, and what remains unknown. If the evidence reveals a wider issue, expand the next task with that evidence. If a hypothesis fails, remove it from the implementation queue.

Review outcomes over a period appropriate to the question, acknowledging seasonality and other releases. A clean before and after crawl establishes changed technical behavior. It does not isolate the cause of every later traffic movement. The guide to [verifying SEO repairs](https://christopherabraham.com/guides/prove-seo-fixes/) covers that distinction.

The deliverable is a short list somebody can act on: an owner, a reason, a prerequisite, and a test for each item. That is what I expect from [a technical SEO audit](https://christopherabraham.com/services/seo-audit/) and from [ongoing SEO support](https://christopherabraham.com/services/seo-retainer/). Progress becomes easier to assess when the queue records decisions instead of accumulating warnings.
