# How to change a business name without losing your Google reviews

By [Christopher Abraham](https://christopherabraham.com/about/) · Published October 7, 2026

A new name feels like a fresh start, and that instinct is exactly what costs businesses their reviews and rankings. The safest rebrand treats the renamed business as the same business it has always been, because to Google it is. Here is the order I follow when a client renames a shop, a restaurant, a studio, or a practice.

## Rule one: edit the listing you have

Your reviews, photos, and history belong to your existing Business Profile, not to your name. If you create a new profile for the new name, you start from zero and leave a second, competing listing behind. Instead, change the name on the profile you already have. Google's help pages say to enter the name exactly as it appears on your real world signage, stationery, and branding, and they warn that changing a verified name may mean verifying again. Plan for that so it doesn't catch you mid launch.

## Rule two: let the real world go first

Google wants the name on the listing to match what customers see. That makes the sequence matter. The new sign, the receipts, the menu or price list, and the website header should carry the new name before or at the same moment the profile changes. If a reviewer at Google, or a customer standing on the sidewalk, sees the old name on the door and the new one online, you've created the kind of mismatch that slows edits down.

## Rule three: don't decorate the name

A rename is a tempting moment to add a keyword or a town to the listing name. Don't. The guidelines ask for the business's real name, and stuffed names are a common reason for suspensions. If the town or the specialty matters, it belongs in the categories, the description, and the website.

## The website and the old name

Search engines and AI assistants will keep meeting your old name for years, in reviews, articles, and directory listings you can't edit. Help them connect the dots.
- Say it plainly on the About page: the business was formerly known as the old name, and when the change happened.
- In your Organization or LocalBusiness structured data, set the new name as `name` and keep the old one as `alternateName`.
- If the domain changes too, redirect every old page to its closest new equivalent with permanent 301 redirects, and keep them in place for the long term.
- Update titles and headings, but don't erase the old name entirely from the site while people still search for it.

## Everywhere else the name lives

Make a list before you start: Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, Tripadvisor or the directories of your industry, booking and delivery platforms, social profiles, and any chamber or association listings. Update the ones you control in the same week as the profile change. For the rest, note who owns them and request corrections. Consistency across these sources is part of how search engines decide that the renamed business is real and the same as before.

## Keep the reviews talking

Reviews stay attached to the profile through a rename, and new ones will mention the new name, which helps. Reply to a few recent reviews after the change; a short reply that uses the new name naturally reinforces the connection without sounding like an announcement.

## Watch the first month

For a few weeks, check that the profile shows the new name, that searches for the old name still find you, and that Search Console isn't reporting a wave of errors from old URLs. Branded searches are the early signal: if people searching either name reach you, the rename worked.

## Tell your best customers yourself

A rename lands better when regulars hear it from you before they notice it on a sign. A short email or a note at the counter, explaining what changed and what didn't, keeps them from wondering whether the business was sold. Those same customers are the ones most likely to leave a fresh review under the new name.

## Common mistakes
- Creating a new profile instead of editing the existing one.
- Changing the listing weeks before the sign goes up.
- Dropping the old domain without redirects.
- Adding keywords to the new name.
- Updating Google and forgetting the rest of the web.
