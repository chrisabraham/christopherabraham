Google Business Profile video verification: plan the take before you press record

By Christopher Abraham · Published October 24, 2026

For a lot of businesses, the only verification option Google offers now is a video recorded on a phone. It looks easy, and it fails for reasons that are easy to avoid: the street wasn't clear, the sign didn't match the profile, or nothing on screen proved the person filming runs the place. Google judges one continuous take, so every one of those mistakes means starting over. This is the checklist I work from, built on what Google's help pages say as of October 2026.

You don't pick the method

Google decides which verification methods a profile gets, and its help page says plainly that they "can't be changed." Depending on the business type and region, the menu can include phone or text, email, a postcard, a live video call with a Google representative, or a recorded video. If the recorded video is the only choice on your screen, that's the one to prepare for.

The rules for the recording itself

  • One continuous take of at least 30 seconds. Google wants it unedited, unique, and complete, with no breaks.
  • Recorded live on a phone, through the Business Profile. Footage shot earlier and uploaded later doesn't count, and neither does anything from a desktop.
  • Nothing sensitive. Keep bank, tax, and ID numbers, other private details, and other people's faces out of the frame. Film before opening or when the shop is empty.

In the app, the path is Get verified, then Business video. Google reviews the upload in up to five business days.

What a storefront or hybrid business has to show

Google treats a storefront as a business that serves customers in person at an address with permanent signage. A hybrid business does that and also travels or delivers. The video needs three things, in this order.

  1. Where you are. Start outside with a street sign, the building number, a neighboring business, or a landmark, so a reviewer can match the spot on Google Maps.
  2. That the business exists there. Pan to the storefront and the sign. Google requires the business name printed on a permanent fixture, a signboard, wall, or window, and it must match the name on the profile. A banner zip-tied to a fence doesn't qualify, and neither does a sign still carrying an old trade name.
  3. That you manage it. Unlock the door, walk in, and show something only staff can reach: the register or point-of-sale screen, the kitchen, the stockroom.

Google's own advice is that a business without permanent signage should be set up as a service-area business instead.

What a service-area business has to show

Plumbers, cleaners, mobile groomers, and every other business that goes to its customers verify differently, and Google says a service-area business shouldn't show its address on the profile at all.

  1. Where you operate from. Street signs or landmarks at the business address. If you work from home, Google says the signs and landmarks in your immediate neighborhood are acceptable. It specifically warns against filming empty land or places with no clear markers.
  2. That the business exists. Professional tools, equipment, stock, a branded vehicle, business cards, or branded uniforms.
  3. That you run it. Perform a piece of the service, or unlock the branded van and show what's inside. Google also accepts a business permit, invoice, or utility bill in the business name, held to the camera with the account numbers covered.

Rehearse it, then record it once

Walk the route before you record, with the phone in hand and the app closed. Decide where you'll start, which sign you'll hold on for a few seconds, which door you'll unlock, and which document you'll show. Do it in daylight. Hold each proof steady long enough to read; a quick swipe past the sign is as good as no sign. Narrating as you go ("this is the corner of Main and Fifth, here is the sign, here is the register") costs nothing and leaves the reviewer less to guess.

Before you start, open the profile and compare it against what the camera will see. The business name, the address, and the category on the profile should match the sign, the building, and the work. A profile name stuffed with keywords that appear on no sign is a mismatch the video will expose.

If the video fails

Google tells you why it rejected the video and offers a Review issues button. The replacement has to include everything from the first recording plus whatever was missing, so record the whole sequence again, start to finish, with the gap fixed. Read the reason closely before trying again; a second video with the same flaw will fail the same way. Google's help page also suggests trying another verification method, if one is offered, before contacting support.

When the reason makes no sense, or the profile fails again and again, the trouble usually sits in the profile itself: a name that doesn't match any document, an address Google associates with another business, a category that doesn't fit what's on camera, or a history of edits that made the listing look unstable. Those need fixing before the next video, or every recording will hit the same wall.

Don't edit the profile mid-verification

Google's help page warns that changing the business name, address, or category while a postcard is on its way makes the code stop working. The same logic applies to every method: an edit hands Google a new set of facts to check. Settle the details first, verify, and make any planned changes afterward, one at a time.
