Fact-checked articles written with AI

AI writes fast and invents freely. It will hand you a quote nobody said, a statistic with no source, and a motive you never had, all in confident prose. I write articles with AI and then check them the way a magazine fact-checker would, so what goes up under your name is in your voice and true.

Starting from your material

Every article starts from something real: a recording, a transcript, an interview, notes, a thread you started, photos you took. The AI drafts from that material. It doesn't get to fill gaps with scenes, quotes, or details that sound plausible. When a gap matters, I ask you instead of guessing.

Your voice, written down

Before the first draft, you and I write down your voice rules: words you'd never use, sentence habits you dislike, how you handle jokes, what tone your readers expect, and how you spell your own name in the possessive. The rules go into every draft and every revision. Generic AI prose has tells, such as hedging, stock transitions, and tidy motivational endings, and the rules screen those out.

The fact-check

Every factual claim is traced to its source before publication:

  • Your own claims are checked against the recording or notes, so the article says exactly what you said.
  • Public facts, such as names, dates, ages, titles, and numbers, are checked against primary or reputable sources, and the article cites them.
  • Other people's words are quoted exactly and attributed to the person who said them.
  • Numbers are reported as what they measure. A Reddit score is not a head count, and a page view is not a reader.
  • Anything unverifiable is cut, attributed, or flagged to you with a note.

You get the article and a short report of what changed in the check and why, with links to the sources.

Built for search and AI answers

Each article gets a search-sized title and description, structured data, an image with real alt text, and internal links to the pages it supports. Google's guidance doesn't penalize content for being written with AI; it targets content that is unhelpful, inaccurate, or mass produced. I covered the details in what Google says about AI-written content. Accurate, sourced articles are also the kind AI assistants cite; see how to correct what AI says about you.

What I won't write

Fake reviews, invented testimonials, articles under a made-up author, and pages mass produced to rank for every variant of a keyword. Each one risks your site and your name.

How it's delivered

Single articles or a monthly number, quoted once the voice rules and the sources are agreed. The first article takes longest, because it sets the rules every later one follows. Terms are on Hire Me.
