AI shopping readiness for small online shops

Shoppers now ask ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode to find the right product and compare a few options. Whether yours makes that short list depends on data most small shops never look at: the feed, the product pages, and the policies a machine reads to decide whether you are a safe recommendation. I check all three and fix what keeps you off the list.

Who this is for

This review fits independent shops on Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, and similar platforms, usually with a few dozen to a few thousand products, run by an owner or a small team. It is also useful just before you apply to Google's early access checkout program or to OpenAI's merchant program, when a clean application matters.

What I review

First the feed. I read your Merchant Center diagnostics and your product data the way the assistants receive it: one row per variant, real stock, prices that match the page, accurate shipping and tax, images of the actual item, and identifiers that hold steady between updates. Then the product pages, checking that titles, descriptions, and structured data agree with the feed and with each other, and that the facts a shopper asks about, such as size, material, compatibility, and care, are written out in text rather than buried in an image.

Next come the trust pages. Shipping, returns, warranty, and contact pages need stable addresses and plain wording, because both OpenAI's feed rules and Google's merchant requirements point to them. Finally I run real shopping questions through ChatGPT and AI Mode, the kind your customers ask, and record whether you appear, how you are described, and which shops appear instead.

What I fix

On your platform I correct feed attributes, variant structure, and product markup, and I rewrite the product facts and policy pages that are thin or contradictory. Where a fix needs a developer or an app change, I write it up as a specific task with the field, the current value, and the correct value. I also add campaign tagging to feed links so shopping traffic from AI assistants shows up separately in your analytics.

Programs and waitlists

I explain which programs fit your store today and which do not.
