Where to get reliable SEO and AI search news in 2026

Search the phrase "best SEO news sites" and the first things you see are ads for AI visibility audits, then an AI summary, then lists of lists. Most of it is marketing for whoever wrote it. Staying current takes a short reading list, an order to read it in, and the habit of checking the claim before repeating it. Here is mine, built over decades of doing this work and refreshed for the AI search era.

Start with the people who make the rules

  • Google Search Central Blog. Official announcements: core updates, policy changes, new Search Console reports. Recent posts covered the site reputation policy update on August 28 and new multimodal performance reporting on September 24.
  • Google Search Status Dashboard. The authoritative record of every ranking update, with start dates and durations. When an article and the dashboard disagree, the dashboard wins.
  • Google's documentation changelog. Quiet but important. On October 1, Google's guidance on AI-generated content began calling manual fact checking and review of AI output critical before publishing.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools and its blog. Bing's AI Performance report is the closest thing to a first-party view of AI citations.
  • The AI companies' own policies. OpenAI's crawler documentation, and usage policies like the one Anthropic updated on October 8, change what is allowed faster than any trade article can summarize.

Then the daily trade press

  • Search Engine Roundtable. Barry Schwartz's site is the fastest on tests, volatility, and small interface changes, often within hours.
  • Search Engine Land. Broad daily coverage and analysis from many contributors. Its footer notes that it is a trademark of Semrush, the SEO software company, which is worth knowing when a story touches SEO tools.
  • Search Engine Journal. News plus long columns from practitioners, published by Alpha Brand Media.

Read at least two for anything big. They often break the same story with different details.

A weekly digest

Aleyda Solis' SEOFOMO newsletter arrives every Sunday with the week's news, guides, tools, and events. If you read only one thing a week, make it this.

Research, with a grain of salt

The big tool makers, Ahrefs and Semrush among them, publish large studies on AI Overviews, citations, and click rates. They are valuable and they are also marketing for the tools that produced the data. Independent practitioners who publish their own tracking, such as Lily Ray and Marie Haynes, are worth following for the same reason: you can see their method and their sample.

Three habits that keep you from repeating mistakes

1. Check dates against the primary record

This month a widely read write-up gave the start of Google's September spam update as "Thursday, September 28." The dashboard says September 24, which was the Thursday. Small slip, easy check, and the kind of error that spreads when everyone copies everyone.

2. Compare studies before quoting one

Three studies this fall measured how often AI Overviews appear for brand searches. Ahrefs reported 83 percent of branded searches; DemandSphere data reported in Search Engine Land showed a jump from 26 to 80 percent; a third found summaries for 93 of 100 brands. Different samples, different methods, same direction. The direction is the finding. The exact number belongs to its study.

3. Read the footnote on every bold claim

One agency's ad this month promises a 100 percent success rate at increasing AI visibility. The footnote defines success as an increase in any one of several visibility metrics from the start of a campaign to its latest measurement. Another vendor reports a "100% client success rate" for making reviews crawlable. Neither claim is false. Both describe something much smaller than the headline suggests.

What to skip

  • "Best agency" lists written by an agency that ranks itself first.
  • Statistics with no named study, sample, or date, especially round ones repeated by AI summaries.
  • Anyone claiming a secret formula for AI Overviews. Google's own documentation says there are no special requirements beyond ordinary search eligibility.
