Google Merchant Center free listings for small shops

Plenty of small store owners assume Google Shopping is only an ad product. Free listings, the unpaid half, shows products from any eligible store at no cost, and many of the shops I audit have never switched it on or have a feed full of errors nobody has read. For a shop with a few dozen to a few thousand products, it's some of the cheapest visibility left in search.

Where free listings appear

Google's Merchant Center help page lists the places: the Shopping tab, product rich results in Search with price, availability, and reviews, Google Images and Lens (including Lens results for similar products), YouTube in some countries, Maps, the products section of a Business Profile, and Gemini. The page is careful to say eligibility doesn't guarantee your products will show. Free listings compete on relevance and data quality, and incomplete data loses.

Setting it up

You need a Merchant Center account, a verified and claimed website, and a product source. On Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, and most hosted platforms, the Google sales channel or an official app builds the feed from your catalog and keeps it current. New Merchant Center accounts are opted in to free listings by default; on an older account, look for the free listings card under Marketing methods and confirm it's on.

A store without a platform integration can upload a spreadsheet feed, but a manual spreadsheet goes stale the first time a price changes. I prefer an automated feed, or, for a small static catalog, letting Merchant Center read the structured data already on the product pages.

The attributes that decide eligibility

Google requires an ID, title, link, image link, price, description, and availability for every product. Depending on the product, it also wants the condition, the brand, a GTIN or manufacturer part number, and whether the item is a multipack or bundle. Clothing needs color, size, age group, and gender.

The GTIN, the barcode number, causes the most confusion. If you resell branded goods, the manufacturer's GTIN belongs in the feed and helps Google match your offer to the product. If you make the products yourself, there's no GTIN to supply, and you mark that the identifier doesn't exist instead of inventing one.

Shipping and returns

In the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany, and several other countries, shipping settings are required. Google also asks for a return policy, ideally set in Merchant Center. These details appear on the listings, and a shopper comparing two stores at the same price will pick the one with free returns shown. Set them once at the account level and they apply to every product.

Fix the Needs attention tab first

The Products page in Merchant Center has a Needs attention tab. That tab is the whole job for the first week. The usual problems I find:

  • Price mismatch. The feed says one price and the page shows another, often because a sale ran on the site and the feed didn't update.
  • Availability mismatch. Products listed in stock that the site says are sold out.
  • Image problems. Promotional overlays, watermarks, or placeholder images.
  • Missing identifiers. Branded products with no GTIN.
  • Policy issues. Restricted products, or a website missing contact details and a return policy.

Each of these has a cause in the store setup. Fixing the cause once beats fixing products one at a time every week.

Write titles for shoppers

The product title in the feed is the line a shopper reads in the results. Lead with what the product is, then the attributes people search by: brand, material, size, color, model. "Walnut cutting board, 18 x 12 inch, end grain" will match more searches than "The Hearthside." Your on-site title can keep the brand flourish while the feed title does the describing.

Connect the pieces

Google's help page recommends linking your Business Profile and any third-party platforms you sell through, and keeping Merchant Center data consistent with your store. Consistency runs in both directions: the product page's structured data, the feed, and the visible page should all carry the same price and availability. When they agree, Google has one answer. When they don't, it has to choose.

How to tell it's working

Merchant Center's performance reports show clicks and impressions from free listings separately from ads. In Search Console, the Performance report's search appearance filter separates merchant listings and product snippets. Give it a few weeks after the errors clear, then compare clicks by product category to see which parts of the catalog Google is showing and which it isn't.

Questions about free listings

Do I need to run Shopping ads?

No. Free listings work on their own. Ads are a separate, paid choice in the same account.

How long until products show?

Google reviews products after the feed is submitted, which can take a few business days. Errors you fix are rechecked on the next feed update.

Do free listings help in AI answers?

Google's help page lists Gemini among the surfaces, and accurate product data is what any shopping assistant needs. Clean feed data is the price of entry.
