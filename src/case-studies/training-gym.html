Private training gym: a brand name Google kept misreading

A members only gym in Northern Virginia, built around private lanes that personal trainers rent for their own clients, had a problem baked into its name: the brand was a single everyday verb. People who searched for it by name, plus the town, were competing with every generic workout query that used the same word.

My part

An hourly engagement of a few hours a week: auditing the website and the gym's presence in Google, cleaning up its Business Profile, rewriting page titles and descriptions, and shaping the local targeting. The owner made the business decisions; I did the search work and reported on it monthly.

Two audiences, one small site

The gym sells to two very different people. One is a member looking for a personal trainer nearby. The other is a trainer looking for a professional space to rent by the hour. The site had to answer both without blurring them, so the service, facility, contact, and FAQ pages each got titles and descriptions written for one clear intent, from "personal trainer" searches in the town to "rent gym space" searches from trainers.

Make the name unambiguous

When a brand is also a common word, Google needs extra help deciding that a search means the business. I made the listing's name, category, address, and description agree exactly with the website and the signage, and removed photos on the profile that showed things the gym didn't actually have. Inaccurate photos are a quiet problem: they tell searchers and Google two different stories about the same place.

Result

While I worked on it, the gym's Business Profile reports showed monthly interactions rising from 269 to 1,019 over about six months. Phone calls went from 7 a month to 56, direction requests from 108 to 754, and website visits from the profile from 153 to 209. In one spring month, Search Console recorded 138 clicks from 3,240 impressions, and the brand plus town search alone brought 40 of those clicks. A new facility was also becoming better known in that period, so I credit the work with removing the confusion rather than with every new member.

What I'd do the same way today

Start with the name and the facts, then the pages. A listing that matches the real business, a site that answers each audience separately, and monthly numbers the owner can read in a minute are still the core of the job. For gyms, studios, and clinics with more than one location, the next step is a page and a profile per location that clearly belong to one company. See local SEO and Google Business Profile.
