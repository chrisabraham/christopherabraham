# Peak season readiness

Some businesses earn most of their year in a few weeks: fireworks before the Fourth, costumes before Halloween, garden centers in spring, ski shops at the first snow. For them, a search problem found during the rush is a problem found too late. I prepare the listings, pages, and measurement months ahead, so the busy weeks run on work that was finished and checked in the quiet ones.

## Who it's for
- Seasonal retailers with one or several locations and a selling window measured in weeks.
- Pop-up and temporary locations that open and close on a calendar.
- Year-round businesses with one dominant season, such as tax preparers or HVAC companies at the first heat wave.

## What gets prepared
- **Listings for every location** on Google, Apple Maps, and Bing, with seasonal hours set as special hours so the profiles never read as closed for good.
- **Location pages** that answer what shoppers ask before they drive: what's in stock, the season's dates and hours, directions, parking, and local rules where they apply.
- **Permanent seasonal pages** that come back at the same address every year, so their history and links carry forward instead of starting over.
- **AI answers** checked for each location: what ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI features say about hours, products, and directions, with errors traced to their sources.
- **The online store**, where there is one: product data, feeds, and structured data consistent with what the stores sell.
- **Measurement** set up before the season, with comparable date windows, so the results can be read honestly afterward.

## How it works

I start from the selling calendar: when customers start researching, when they buy, and when each location opens and closes. Work is scheduled backward from those dates, with substantial changes finished during the quiet months and a cutoff agreed before the rush. During the season the job narrows to watching for errors that cost sales, such as wrong hours, a broken store locator, or a listing marked closed, and fixing them as they appear.

After the season I set what happened beside the baseline and write down what to keep, what to change, and what to start earlier next year.

## What I'll need from you

The selling calendar for each location, including opening and closing dates; access to your Business Profiles, Search Console, and analytics; the list of products or services that sell in the season; and last season's numbers if you kept them. A list of what went wrong last year is just as useful, because the worst surprises tend to repeat.
