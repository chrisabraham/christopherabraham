# SEO report review

Every month a report arrives with charts that go up and a list of tasks marked done. You pay the invoice and can't tell whether the work is helping. I read the report the way an auditor reads a set of books: every claim checked against your own Search Console, analytics, and the live site, and a written answer about which parts hold up, which don't, and what's missing.

## When owners ask for this
- Traffic or leads have been flat for months while the reports say things are improving.
- A new agency delivered a long audit and you can't tell which items matter.
- You're about to renew a contract, raise a budget, or switch providers.
- A board member or investor asked how search is going and you'd like an outside answer.

## What I check
- **The numbers.** Do the clicks, impressions, and rankings in the report match what your own Search Console and analytics show for the same dates and the same pages? Are year-over-year comparisons using matching windows?
- **The metrics chosen.** Reports full of vanity numbers, such as total keywords ranked or a domain score from a third-party tool, can rise while revenue-earning pages decline. I look at the pages and queries that bring your customers.
- **The work claimed.** If the report says titles were rewritten, redirects fixed, or schema added, I check the live pages to see it.
- **The audit findings.** For a long technical audit, I sort the findings into the few that change outcomes, the many that are housekeeping, and any that are wrong.
- **What's missing.** Problems the report doesn't mention: indexing issues, a broken inquiry form, AI answers getting your business wrong.
- **Links and risk.** If the report counts new backlinks, I look at where they came from, because bought links are a risk for you, not the agency.

## What you get

A written review in plain English: each major claim in the report, what your data shows, and my verdict. A short list of the questions to put to your agency. And, where the work is sound, I say so, because a good agency deserves the renewal. My guide to [proving an SEO fix worked](https://christopherabraham.com/guides/prove-seo-fixes/) shows the standard of evidence I hold reports to, and [what an SEO audit should deliver](https://christopherabraham.com/guides/seo-audit-deliverables/) covers what a good audit looks like.

## What I need from you

The report or audit itself, read access to Search Console and your analytics, and the contract or statement of work so I know what was promised. Read-only access is enough; I don't change anything on your site during a review.
